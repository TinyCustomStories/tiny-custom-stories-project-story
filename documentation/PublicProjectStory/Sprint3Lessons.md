# Sprint 3: progress, friction, and a smaller checkpoint

Snapshot: October 16, 2026 (UTC). This is a curated retrospective, not a complete session audit, productivity benchmark, or release assessment.

## What the history supports

Local Codex founder-message history and selected full sessions from October 2 to 15 show a recurring pattern. The founder first asked for more executable work and concurrency, then asked why original outcomes stayed blocked, why Story Studio was not progressing in parent-journey order, and why new task creation appeared to move the finish line. Later requests focused on stopped sessions, open PRs, shared verification delays, and fresh contexts with useful handoffs.

The October 7 journey-order discussion and October 8 queue discussion support the distinction between independent component delivery and a connected experience. The October 14 scope conversation identified optional-context and source-lifecycle complexity as a major contributor and led to an explicitly smaller supported path. The October 15 workflow conversation proposed stronger-model planning, cheaper execution, and separate review; a mandatory extra planning stage was still a proposal. Existing capability-based routing remains the adopted baseline.

The website paraphrases those pain points. Raw messages, local session identifiers, private issue text, source paths, family data, and diagnostic logs are intentionally excluded. A founder-reported historical task-growth figure is not republished as an independently verified count. No model speedup, API saving, or causal estimate of time lost is claimed.

## What changed in delivery

Queue preparation now starts with unfinished accepted outcomes and real prerequisites, with completion reconciliation before new slices. Small tasks remain useful when each has a path back to closing the original outcome. Separate writers, independent review, compact source-bound handoffs, and coordination of shared verification resources address different problems; more sessions alone do not establish faster delivery.

The accepted October 14 scope decision and its dated amendments authorize one intermediate fictional, profile-only Fun & Fantasy checkpoint: saved name and age, fresh plan acceptance, deterministic cover plus ten text pages, essential finite checks, exact-revision approval, and identical immutable protected reopen. Optional source context, custom instructions, alternative protagonists, editing, and rewrites are unavailable within this checkpoint. Server enforcement matters as much as hidden controls.

The checkpoint is not usable Alpha, broad suitability certification, a completed sprint or epic, child publication, or real-family provider/release approval. Safety, authorization, isolation, currentness, recovery, and immutable approval remain required. Deferred source-lifecycle work remains valid future work.
